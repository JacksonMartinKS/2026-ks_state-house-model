House races vs the federal baseline

Current district lines (2022 map). Expected = the district's presidential lean vs the nation (same year, or the prior one in a midterm) + generic ballot × elasticity + incumbency. Each miss feeds the candidate ratings, fit jointly with the statewide races.

Each district card uses the Senate race's method: baseline partisanship is the district's 2024 presidential margin relative to the nation, the full generic-ballot shift applies, and candidate ratings come from the same joint fit. Campaign finance uses FEC candidate spending with a $625K visibility threshold (the statewide $2.5M split across four districts).

Baseline partisanship

Senate: Kansas 2024 presidential lean vs the nation. Governor: average lean of governor races. Other state offices: average lean of past statewide-office results.

Candidate ratings

R−D points; negative = ran ahead for Democrats or behind for Republicans. Fit from every 2014–2024 race's miss vs its baseline; older races count less (weight halves every 8 years), and each miss is credited by candidate profile.

Candidate profiles: who a race's miss is credited to

Judgment calls. Each race's miss vs its baseline is split between the two candidates in proportion to their prior variance. High-profile candidates get 1.5× the usual spread and low-profile or underfunded ones 0.5×, so a race against a well-known opponent is read mostly as that opponent's result (a High vs Low race splits about 90 / 10). Everyone else splits evenly.
